# Draft

## Draft

Comment voice is not post voice. Pick one move. Keys stay fixed so later counts still line up:

- `plain_opinion`: a take they share, or one they doubt.

- `plain_answer`: the post asked something.

- `honest_opinion`: no clean answer exists.

- `next_step`: a play they also run.

- `what_i_use`: a tool post or a workflow post.

- `real_question`: they want that person's method.

- `disagree`: push back only from work they have done.

- `agree_angle`: they agree, then add one angle.

- `my_story`: the direct parallel.

- `joke`: they can stand behind it.

- `peer_win`: someone they know just shipped or grew.

On a two-draft card the moves differ, one of the two is a single line, and the first slot is not always the same move. The pick should show the move, not the slot.

Write it the way they type on a phone. Cut the setup, the summary, and any word that only sounds smart. Start from what caught them. If the line would fit any other post, if it grades the post, or if it sounds like a tip, write it again.

Defaults until a measured shape replaces them: one line, between 6 and 30 words, and the batch is mostly single lines. A second short paragraph is only an honest opening plus an opinion, or a reaction plus one detail from their own work. A numbered list is only for steps that answer the post's question.

Two or more sentences get a blank line between them. Ask only when they would actually want the reply. Cap questions at 4 in 10. About half the batch may use a laugh, an emoji, lowercase, or odd punctuation. Never the whole batch.

Taste to test, then replace from their verdicts: prefer a concrete practice, a tool, or a result over a soft take. For a technical post, name the change they would make, including which tool, which order, and the cost reason, instead of only asking.

On a hype or results post, react in their words first, then one result if they have one. Skip a setup tour unless that exact setup is the subject. A failure story is not a general hook. It belongs only on the same subject. Lines they actually say, including a slightly sharp one, stay. A sanded-down take gets skipped.
