# Opener

On the opener, use short phrase captions, real supporting pictures, small camera moves, and only a few motion graphics. Match each picture to the beat they are saying. A launch page, a logo, or a number shows up when they name it. It stays about 1.5 to 3 seconds, then leaves.

Show the real picture from the recording or the receipt. The opener still works with the sound off. The speaker remains the subject. Keep those phrase captions a comfortable size and clear of the face. Nudge the framing at the boundary between phrases. One graphic carries one spoken idea, then it leaves.

When a counter, a chart, and an arrow share one reveal, they match on every frame. Use an analytics picture or a chart drawn from real measurements. That analytics picture is full frame and wears the numbers already printed on it.

If the figure has a window, say the window, for example the last 7 days. Numbers and wording on screen match the speech and the Proof page. Give each related figure its own beat. A comparison becomes columns, one figure to a column.

Hold one calm system through the opener: neutral ground, a few see-through panels, precise type, and a single accent.

Vary the side so the opener does not sit in one place: left card, then the whole frame, then a right card, then the whole frame again, then a split.

Shift the camera away from whichever side the card occupies. Where type sits on a dark or busy picture, thicken the glass.

A full-frame moment is frosted glass over their own footage. Ramp the blur in and back out. `DeskTimeline` sets `backdrop-filter` on the frost and drives a separate tint layer.

That frosted full frame can also cover a jump cut.

For a longer stretch, snap-punch in on the phrase that matters, or split the frame: the speaker inside a rounded window, a diagram building beside them, the two at one height.

The first frame is already moving, a small zoom that settles. On the closing line, push closer, then cut into the demonstration.
