# Opener

On the opener, use short phrase captions, real supporting pictures, small camera moves, and only a few motion graphics. Match each picture to the beat they are saying. A launch page, a logo, or a number shows up when they name it. It stays about 1.5 to 3 seconds, then leaves.

Show the real picture from the recording or the receipt. Do not draw a fake card. The opener still works with the sound off. The speaker remains the subject. Keep those phrase captions a comfortable size and clear of the face. Nudge the framing at the boundary between phrases. One graphic carries one spoken idea, then it leaves.

A counter may climb only as far as the real value. When a counter, a chart, and an arrow share one reveal, they match on every frame. Use an analytics picture or a chart drawn from real measurements. That analytics picture is full frame and wears the numbers already printed on it.

Do not stamp a rounder caption over it.

If the figure has a window, say the window, for example the last 7 days. Numbers and wording on screen match the speech and the Proof page. When the speech and a receipt disagree, put the receipt on screen and tell the owner. Give each related figure its own beat. A comparison becomes columns, one figure to a column.

Hold one calm system through the opener: neutral ground, a few see-through panels, precise type, and a single accent.

Do not use captions that are large and all the same, slabs that crowd the frame, wipes that look cheap, or transitions that bounce.

Vary the side so the opener does not sit in one place: left card, then the whole frame, then a right card, then the whole frame again, then a split.

Shift the camera away from whichever side the card occupies. Where type sits on a dark or busy picture, thicken the glass.

A full-frame moment is frosted glass over their own footage. Ramp the blur in and back out. `DeskTimeline` sets `backdrop-filter` on the frost and drives a separate tint layer.

Do not fade the overlay parent. If that parent sits below opacity 1, the blur switches off and the frost arrives late. That frosted full frame can also cover a jump cut.

Do not let a talking stretch run past about 7 seconds with no visual change. For a longer stretch, snap-punch in on the phrase that matters, or split the frame: the speaker inside a rounded window, a diagram building beside them, the two at one height.

The first frame is already moving, a small zoom that settles. On the closing line, push closer, then cut into the demonstration.
