description: "Build a packaging brief from outlier evidence the owner can support: two titles for a split, every claim marked, and thumbnail concepts. Use before the outline. It does not upload, and it does not invent proof."
---

Follow `docs/skill-frame.md`.

The brief is what this pass prepares. The owner picks what ships. Nothing here uploads, and nothing here invents proof. The brief holds measured outlier references, two titles for a split, and thumbnail concepts.

Before any number that would be public, read the Proof page. A figure that is not already cleared there is not the owner's to claim. The title has to stop the reader named in `audience.local/ideal-follower.md`. If a week brief is already filled (`vault/week/<date>.md`, or `vault/week/brief.md`), package to that brief. Read the reference set with `thumb-patterns`. After the owner chooses, hand those concepts to `thumb-frame` for the render.

A number inside a title stays packaging until the owner can show that number in the video. Do not invent a metric. Do not pass another channel's result off as this owner's. Do not force a three-way comparison unless the video itself is a comparison. Set both titles before any outline exists. That outline then has to keep the promise those titles made.

Outliers, only from evidence they supply or from a read that returns posts. Before hand-typed counts, resolve `source.account_posts_youtube` with `tables.executable_definitions.list_resolved`. It is a definition id, not a `tools_run` id. Attach with `tables.table_sources.attach`, then run `tables.table_sources.run` only after the quote and the owner's yes. If that definition is not resolved, they type the counts. Take what you actually hold, in this order:

1. Outlier scores and trend views they already pasted, including a set that spans several channels. Do not assume a browser ranking plugin is installed.
2. A hand pass over the niche for the last three to six months, or that same read. This desk does not call a video-host API.
3. Channels the reader already watches.

Call a video an outlier when it sits well ahead of the rest of its own channel. Compare current views with the median of that channel's uploads of similar age and similar length, and only when at least five of those peers exist. Without those peers, the ratio is not evidence. The ratio is views as they stand now. It is not views per hour. It is not a click-through rate. It is not evidence that the wording produced the views.

Then screen the rows yourself. Drop livestreams, anything under four minutes, and off-audience topics. Read the description and note a sponsor. Open the real thumbnail. Record the words on it, whether the subject is a face or an interface, and the one promise that picture makes. Keep every URL. Each row also carries the views, the peer median, and the ratio they supplied. If those figures are missing, leave the ratio blank rather than filling one in. Shortlist five to ten.

Before a structure enters the pair, it has to clear the outside-niche test. That test is `video-pick` gate 2. It looks across four to six donor niches. Keep the structure only when evidence they provided shows at least 3× from three or more channels in two or more niches, and the home niche has not already worn that shape out. If the evidence is missing, label the structure a hypothesis and do not treat it as validated.

Take the shape. Leave their sentences behind.

| Structure | Shape | Fits when |
|---|---|---|
| Visible test | A trial the viewer can watch, or a question that asks for a verdict | The video runs the test and shows the verdict |
| Too-easy outcome | The result is cast as almost unfairly simple | The outcome is strong, and the video really shows it |
| Counted roster | A count of items, or a tally set off in parentheses | The roster is on screen |
| Timeboxed volume | How much work, locked to a time box | Speed is the story |
| Before-to-after | A start state, then the state after the change | One visual consequence |
| Exact figure | A precise figure, where a rounded one would be weaker | Only when the video shows that figure |
| Reversal | A capability stated, then why the outcome still fails | A problem set up for a new viewer |

Skip the shape that treats a topic as if it had gone unsaid. If the buyer is a founder, leave job-ladder titles out.

Name the real outcome in one line. Pick two different structures, not two phrasings of one hook. Put a risky claim, or a numeric one, in parentheses or in the second clause, so that piece can be replaced on its own. On every claim, write one label: proven, on-video-only, or hypothesis.

- Proven: the owner can show it, and any number is already on the Proof page.
- On-video-only: the footage will show it, and it is not a standing public metric.
- Hypothesis: the video does not show it yet. Packaging only.

Write Variant A, then Variant B. Give each a line on why that structure fits this video.

Judge the thumbnail ideas at 320×180. From the shortlist, note where the words sit, whether you see a face or a screen, how large a numeral is, the colour, and which element the eye reaches first. Write four to six concepts as text, visual, colour, and what not to repeat. Put one promise on each. Put two to five words on the image.

Return the shortlist with URLs, both titles with claim status, the concepts, and whatever the owner still has to confirm before publishing.
